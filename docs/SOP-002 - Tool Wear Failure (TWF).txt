DOCUMENT ID: SOP-002
TITLE: Tool Wear Failure (TWF) Detection and Tool Replacement Procedure
FAILURE CODE: TWF
APPLIES TO: Drill bits, milling cutters, and lathe inserts
SEVERITY: Medium to High

1. PURPOSE
Provide a standard procedure for identifying excessive tool wear, safely removing the worn tool, and preparing the machine for continued operation.

2. FAILURE DESCRIPTION
TWF occurs when the cutting implement has degraded sufficiently that normal machining performance can no longer be maintained reliably. Progressive wear may increase torque demand and reduce process stability.

3. SYMPTOMS / TRIGGER CONDITIONS
- Tool Wear metric exceeds 200 minutes of continuous operation.
- Gradual, unexplained increases in Torque are required to maintain Rotational Speed.
- Cutting performance may deteriorate as the tool becomes dull or damaged.
- Metal shavings or visible tool damage may be present during inspection.

4. LIKELY ROOT CAUSES
- Physical degradation from prolonged material friction.
- Dulling of the cutting edge.
- Micro-fracturing or other physical damage to the cutting implement.
- Continued operation beyond the intended tool-wear interval.

5. IMMEDIATE MITIGATION
1. Halt the current feed cycle and retract the tool arm from the material.
2. Lock the spindle and initiate the automated tool-release sequence.
3. Inspect the chuck for metal shavings or debris before inserting the replacement tool.
4. Replace the worn or damaged tool using the approved tool-change procedure.
5. Reset the Tool Wear timer to 0 in the edge monitoring system before resuming the batch.

6. DIAGNOSTIC CHECKS
- Review Tool Wear history and current timer value.
- Check Torque trend against recent normal operation.
- Visually inspect the cutting tool for dulling, chips, cracks, or micro-fractures.
- Check the chuck and tool holder for debris or abnormal wear.

7. ESCALATION CONDITION
Escalate to maintenance engineering if excessive torque remains after tool replacement, if the replacement tool shows abnormal wear immediately, or if the tool holder/chuck is damaged.

8. RESTART CONDITIONS
Resume operation only after the replacement tool is securely installed, the spindle/tool assembly is cleared, and the Tool Wear timer has been reset.

9. SAFETY NOTES
Follow machine-specific tool-change, spindle-locking, and guarding procedures. Do not access the cutting zone while the machine is capable of unexpected movement.

10. RELATED PARAMETERS
Tool Wear, Torque, Rotational Speed, feed-cycle state, spindle state.
